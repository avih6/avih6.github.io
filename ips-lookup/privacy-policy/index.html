1. Overview & Privacy-First Principle

The "IP Lookup" application (the "App") is developed and maintained by avih6 as a free, ad-supported mobile network utility. This SERVICE is provided at no cost and is intended for use "AS IS".

The App does not require user accounts or ask for names, phone numbers, or login credentials to perform lookups. avih6 does not operate a backend that receives or stores the App’s IP lookup requests, and the App does not save a search history. The App communicates directly with the third-party services described below; those services may process information under their own policies. If you contact support, avih6 receives the information you choose to send.

2. Network Intelligence Processing & Local Data Handling

2.1 IP Geolocation Lookups (ipwho.is API)

To provide network intelligence, ISP lookup, ASN verification, and approximate city/country geolocation, the App sends lookup requests directly over HTTPS to ipwho.is. A lookup includes the IP address entered by the user; on startup, the service determines the device’s public IP from the request. The App does not associate lookups with a user account. Map tiles for approximate geographic coordinates are requested from OpenStreetMap community servers over HTTPS. These providers receive the connection’s public IP and the requested IP or map tile information, as applicable. HTTPS protects data in transit; it does not determine whether a provider logs or retains requests. Provider processing and retention are governed by their own policies.

2.2 Local Network Inspection & Device Permissions

• Local Wi-Fi State: The App requests permission to view Wi-Fi state (android.permission.ACCESS_WIFI_STATE) and general network state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE) strictly to read and display local gateway details (such as local Wi-Fi SSID and private IPv4/IPv6 gateway addresses) directly on the device interface.

• Haptic Feedback: The App uses the device vibrator (android.permission.VIBRATE) to provide tactile feedback when copying network data, completing a successful lookup, and interacting with settings.

2.3 Search History & Local Preferences

The App does not save a history of IP searches. Lookup results are held in memory for display. Only user preferences, such as theme, haptic feedback, live monitoring, and language settings, are persisted by the App using Jetpack DataStore in its private device storage. No search history or lookup results are sent to a developer-operated database.

2.4 User-Initiated Sharing & Support

Copying a value places it on the Android clipboard. Sharing a diagnostic report sends the selected report text to an app chosen by the user. Opening a location in a maps app passes the displayed approximate coordinates to that app. These actions occur when the user selects them, and the receiving app handles the information under its own policies.

The support action opens an email composer with the App’s name and version, Android version, and device manufacturer and model. The user can review or edit the message before sending it. If sent, avih6 receives the sender’s email address, the message, and any information the sender includes, for responding to the support request.

5. User Rights & Local Data Management

The App has no user-account database or developer-operated lookup-history database. Local preferences and information processed by external providers are managed separately:

  • Local Data Removal: You can clear the App’s locally stored preferences through Android OS Settings. There is no saved search history to delete. Clearing App storage does not delete records that third-party providers may retain.
  • Third-Party Data: Advertising, diagnostic, and request information processed by providers is subject to their own retention practices and privacy controls. Consult their policies for information about available choices and requests.
  • Privacy Inquiries: Users may direct any privacy inquiries to av6development@gmail.com.

6. Data Security

Security of user information is prioritized, and commercially reasonable technical and organizational safeguards are employed. However, no method of electronic storage or internet transmission is 100% immune to vulnerabilities, and absolute security cannot be guaranteed.
